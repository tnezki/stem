STEM II – ES2-II: Develop and Justify Engineering Solutions

Essential Standard

Students will generate, evaluate, and justify engineering solutions using evidence, trade-offs, and design criteria in order to select design approaches that are purposeful, feasible, and aligned to challenge goals.
Learning Target 1: Generating Multiple Design Ideas

Learning Target: Students will develop multiple possible solutions that respond to the engineering problem, criteria, constraints, and scientific principles of the challenge.

Challenge Connections: Bridge, Glider, Earthquake Tower, MagLev Vehicle, Landing Rover, Water Rocket Distance, Design Your Own Challenge

  • I can generate multiple possible solutions to an engineering problem.
  • I can create sketches, diagrams, or written descriptions that communicate design ideas.
  • I can use challenge criteria and constraints to guide brainstorming.
  • I can identify advantages and possible limitations of different design ideas.
  • I can build on research, prior examples, and teammate ideas to improve possible solutions.
Learning Target 2: Evaluating Design Alternatives

Learning Target: Students will compare design alternatives by using criteria, constraints, evidence, trade-offs, and expected performance.

Challenge Connections: Bridge, Glider, Earthquake Tower, MagLev Vehicle, Landing Rover

  • I can compare multiple design options using the goals of the challenge.
  • I can identify trade-offs between performance, cost, reliability, stability, safety, and efficiency.
  • I can use a decision matrix or structured comparison to evaluate alternatives.
  • I can explain why one design may be stronger than another for a specific challenge.
  • I can revise my design thinking when evidence shows that an idea may not meet the requirements.
Learning Target 3: Justifying Engineering Decisions

Learning Target: Students will defend design choices by explaining how evidence, research, criteria, constraints, and trade-offs influenced the selected solution.

Challenge Connections: Bridge, MagLev Vehicle, Landing Rover, Water Rocket Distance, Design Your Own Challenge

  • I can explain why a design was selected over other possible solutions.
  • I can defend engineering decisions using criteria, constraints, and evidence.
  • I can justify trade-offs made during planning, building, or redesign.
  • I can connect design features to expected performance outcomes.
  • I can communicate design reasoning clearly enough for others to understand and critique.

Progress Tracking

Not Yet: I need support generating multiple solutions or explaining why one idea may work better than another.
Getting There: I can compare designs but need more practice using evidence, criteria, and trade-offs to justify decisions.
Got It!: I can generate, evaluate, and justify engineering solutions using evidence and challenge requirements.

Assessment Methods by DOK Level

DOK 1 — Rungs 1–2

Format: 3–5 minute warm-up, brainstorming prompt, sketch prompt, quick check, idea sort, mini-whiteboard response, or exit ticket

Purpose: Check whether students can recognize that engineering problems have multiple possible solutions and can generate more than one reasonable design idea.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Multiple Solution Brainstorm Can Be Assessed During Any Major Challenge – Students generate several possible solutions before choosing one direction.
  2. Bridge Design Idea Sort Can Be Assessed During Bridge – Students identify different bridge design approaches and describe one possible strength of each.
  3. Glider Sketch Check Can Be Assessed During Glider – Students sketch two possible glider layouts and label features that may affect flight.
  4. Design Feature Recognition Can Be Assessed During MagLev or Landing Rover – Students match design features to intended purposes such as stability, protection, lift, control, or efficiency.
What this tells the teacher:
Students who struggle here may jump to the first idea without considering alternatives. They may need structured brainstorming, example analysis, sketch support, or reminders that engineering design usually improves when multiple possible solutions are considered first.
DOK 2 — Rungs 3–4

Format: Exit ticket, design comparison, decision matrix, partner check, ranking task, trade-off chart, or short written explanation

Purpose: Check whether students can compare design alternatives using challenge criteria, constraints, and expected performance.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Bridge Design Comparison Matrix Can Be Assessed During Bridge – Students compare bridge concepts using strength, cost, material efficiency, and build feasibility.
  2. Glider Trade-Off Task Can Be Assessed During Glider – Students compare two glider designs and identify trade-offs involving distance, hang time, stability, and construction quality.
  3. Landing Rover Protection vs. Cost Analysis Can Be Assessed During Landing Rover – Students evaluate how different material choices affect protection, cost, mass, and size.
  4. MagLev Design Alternative Ranking Can Be Assessed During MagLev Vehicle – Students rank design options using momentum goals, cargo capacity, stability, friction reduction, and propulsion effectiveness.
What this tells the teacher:
Students at this level may be able to describe designs but not yet compare them systematically. The assessment reveals whether students can use criteria and constraints as decision tools rather than choosing based only on appearance, preference, or team momentum.
DOK 3 — Rungs 5–6

Format: Constructed response, decision defense, trade-off analysis, design review, peer critique, CER-style explanation, or group whiteboard defense

Purpose: Check whether students can justify engineering decisions using evidence, criteria, constraints, and trade-offs.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Decision Matrix Defense Can Be Assessed During Bridge or Earthquake Tower – Students use a decision matrix to defend the design they selected and explain the trade-offs involved.
  2. MagLev Design Justification Can Be Assessed During MagLev Vehicle – Students justify how their design choices support levitation, cargo mass, velocity, and momentum goals.
  3. Landing Rover Trade-Off Explanation Can Be Assessed During Landing Rover – Students explain why their team prioritized certain materials or protective features over others.
  4. Water Rocket Distance Design Defense Can Be Assessed During Water Rocket Distance – Students defend design choices related to fins, nose cone, bottle size, weight, budget, and launch consistency.
What this tells the teacher:
This level shows whether students can move from “we picked this” to “we picked this because…” Strong responses name specific criteria, acknowledge trade-offs, and connect design features to expected or observed performance.
DOK 4 — Rungs 7–8

Format: Performance task, design proposal, critique task, unfamiliar design scenario, design review board, capstone planning document, or engineering defense

Purpose: Check whether students can transfer ES2 skills by generating, evaluating, defending, or critiquing solutions in complex or unfamiliar engineering situations.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Critique a Design Decision Can Be Assessed After Multiple Major Challenges – Students evaluate whether a design choice is supported by the stated criteria, constraints, and evidence.
  2. Unfamiliar Challenge Design Proposal Can Be Assessed Before Design Your Own Challenge – Students generate multiple possible solutions for a new challenge and defend the strongest option.
  3. Design Review Board Defense Can Be Assessed During Design Your Own Challenge – Students present design alternatives, justify the selected approach, and respond to critique or questions.
  4. Capstone Solution Selection Rationale Can Be Assessed During Design Your Own Challenge – Students document why their final capstone design is the best fit for their self-created criteria, constraints, and testing system.
What this tells the teacher:
DOK 4 tasks show whether students can think like engineers before building. Strong students can generate options, evaluate them fairly, defend decisions, respond to critique, and adapt reasoning when constraints or evidence change.

Summative Assessment Plan

Summative Evidence for ES2-II: Students demonstrate proficiency by producing an engineering artifact that shows multiple design ideas, comparison of alternatives, trade-off analysis, and justification for a selected solution.

Recommended Artifact Options:

  • Decision Matrix Report: A written artifact comparing design alternatives using weighted criteria, evidence, and a final design justification.
  • Engineering Sketch Portfolio: A collection of annotated sketches showing multiple ideas, design features, trade-offs, and final selection reasoning.
  • Blueprint Package: A technical artifact showing the selected design, measurements, materials, design rationale, and expected performance.
  • Design Review Board Defense: A verbal or recorded defense where students present alternatives, justify their selected solution, and respond to critique.
  • Engineering Design Proposal: A formal proposal explaining the problem, possible solutions, final design choice, trade-offs, and expected performance.
  • Design Justification Video: A short video explaining why the selected design best fits the criteria and constraints of the challenge.

Challenge Examples: Bridge Decision Matrix Report, Glider Engineering Sketch Portfolio, Earthquake Tower Blueprint Package, MagLev Design Review Board Defense, Landing Rover Design Proposal, Water Rocket Distance Design Justification Video, or Design Your Own Challenge Solution Selection Rationale.

Suggested Rubric Categories: number and variety of design ideas, quality of sketches or descriptions, use of criteria and constraints, trade-off analysis, decision matrix or comparison quality, evidence-based justification, clarity of final design rationale, and ability to respond to critique.

Holistic Rubric

A — Highly Proficient

The student generates multiple thoughtful design solutions, evaluates alternatives using criteria and constraints, clearly explains trade-offs, and defends the selected solution with strong evidence and engineering reasoning. The artifact shows purposeful decision-making and a clear connection between design features and challenge goals.

B — Proficient

The student generates multiple possible solutions, compares alternatives, and provides a reasonable justification for the selected design. The artifact uses criteria, constraints, and some evidence to explain design decisions, though some trade-offs or explanations may need more depth.

C — Developing Proficiency

The student shows partial understanding of solution development and justification. The artifact may include more than one idea, but comparisons may be basic, evidence may be limited, or the final design choice may not be fully justified. There is enough evidence that the student understands that engineering decisions should be based on more than preference.

Not Yet — Insufficient Evidence

The student does not yet provide sufficient evidence of ES2-II proficiency. The artifact may show only one design idea, weak comparison of alternatives, missing trade-off analysis, or little evidence-based justification. The student may need reteaching, structured brainstorming, decision matrix support, peer critique, or reassessment.
