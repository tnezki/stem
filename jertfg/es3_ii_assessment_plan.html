STEM II – ES3-II: Build, Test, and Analyze Prototypes

Essential Standard

Students will construct prototypes, collect and analyze performance data, and evaluate design effectiveness in order to determine how well a design meets the challenge goals and what evidence should guide future decisions.
Learning Target 1: Constructing Functional Prototypes

Learning Target: Students will build prototypes that follow design plans, meet challenge requirements, use materials effectively, and remain testable under the conditions of the challenge.

Challenge Connections: Bridge, Glider, Earthquake Tower, MagLev Vehicle, Landing Rover, Water Rocket Distance

  • I can construct a prototype that matches the selected design plan and challenge requirements.
  • I can use materials, tools, and class time effectively during the build process.
  • I can make construction decisions that improve stability, reliability, safety, or performance.
  • I can identify build problems that prevent a prototype from being tested fairly.
  • I can document important construction choices so another person can understand how the prototype was built.
Learning Target 2: Collecting Performance Data

Learning Target: Students will develop and follow testing procedures, collect accurate measurements, and record observations that describe how a prototype performs.

Challenge Connections: Bridge, Glider, Earthquake Tower, MagLev Vehicle, Landing Rover, Water Rocket Distance

  • I can follow a testing procedure that matches the scoring system and challenge goals.
  • I can collect accurate quantitative data such as distance, time, mass, height, load, cost, speed, momentum, or number of successful trials.
  • I can record qualitative observations about stability, consistency, failure points, control, protection, or reliability.
  • I can conduct multiple trials when needed to improve the usefulness of the data.
  • I can identify variables that may affect the fairness or reliability of a test.
Learning Target 3: Analyzing Prototype Performance

Learning Target: Students will interpret testing results, identify patterns, evaluate strengths and weaknesses, and use evidence to explain how well a prototype meets the design criteria.

Challenge Connections: Bridge, Glider, Earthquake Tower, MagLev Vehicle, Landing Rover, Water Rocket Distance, Design Your Own Challenge

  • I can organize test results in a way that makes performance easy to interpret.
  • I can identify patterns, outliers, strengths, weaknesses, and failure points in testing data.
  • I can compare expected performance with actual results and explain important differences.
  • I can use data and observations to evaluate whether a prototype meets the challenge criteria.
  • I can explain prototype performance using evidence, scientific principles, and engineering reasoning.

Progress Tracking

Not Yet: I need support building a testable prototype or identifying what data should be collected.
Getting There: I can build and test a prototype but need more practice collecting reliable data and explaining what the results mean.
Got It!: I can build, test, organize data, and explain prototype performance using evidence.

Assessment Methods by DOK Level

DOK 1 — Rungs 1–2

Format: 3–5 minute warm-up, quick check, test-plan prompt, image prompt, build-readiness check, or exit ticket

Purpose: Check whether students can recognize the purpose of testing, identify what should be measured, and connect basic performance evidence to challenge goals.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Prototype Testing Purpose Check Can Be Assessed During Any Major Challenge – Students explain why a prototype must be tested before design claims can be made.
  2. Measurement Match Can Be Assessed During Glider or MagLev – Students match challenge goals to measurements such as distance, time, speed, momentum, mass, or successful trials.
  3. Build Readiness Checklist Can Be Assessed Before Initial Testing – Students identify whether a prototype is complete, safe, stable, and ready for a fair test.
  4. Observation or Measurement Sort Can Be Assessed During Bridge, Landing Rover, or Earthquake Tower – Students sort evidence into quantitative measurements and qualitative observations.
What this tells the teacher:
Students who struggle here may see testing as a competition only rather than a way to gather evidence. They may need support identifying measurable outcomes, distinguishing observations from data, or understanding why test conditions must be clear.
DOK 2 — Rungs 3–4

Format: Exit ticket, data table task, testing checklist, partner check, graph or table interpretation, or short written response

Purpose: Check whether students can collect, organize, and interpret basic performance data from prototype tests.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Bridge Efficiency Data Table Can Be Assessed During Bridge – Students record cost, load supported, and efficiency data, then describe what the results show about performance.
  2. Glider Flight Trial Analysis Can Be Assessed During Glider – Students organize distance, time, stability, and landing data from multiple trials to identify the strongest performance.
  3. Earthquake Tower Test Results Can Be Assessed During Earthquake Tower – Students interpret testing phases, number of floors, budget data, and survival results to evaluate prototype success.
  4. Landing Rover Drop Test Data Can Be Assessed During Landing Rover – Students organize drop results, egg survival, cost, and failure observations to determine whether the rover meets the challenge criteria.
What this tells the teacher:
Students at this level can usually run tests, but the assessment reveals whether their data is accurate, organized, and connected to the challenge goals. Weak responses often include missing units, incomplete trials, unclear observations, or conclusions that do not match the data.
DOK 3 — Rungs 5–6

Format: Constructed response, data analysis paragraph, failure-point analysis, graph explanation, CER-style response, or group whiteboard defense

Purpose: Check whether students can analyze testing evidence and explain why a prototype performed the way it did.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Prototype Performance CER Can Be Assessed During Any Major Challenge – Students make a claim about how well a prototype performed and support it with test data and reasoning.
  2. Structural Failure Point Analysis Can Be Assessed During Bridge or Earthquake Tower – Students identify the first major failure point and explain what design feature likely caused it.
  3. Expected vs. Actual Results Can Be Assessed During Glider, MagLev, or Water Rocket Distance – Students compare predicted performance with actual data and explain why the results may have differed.
  4. Data Pattern Explanation Can Be Assessed During Landing Rover or MagLev – Students identify a pattern across trials and explain how a design feature, variable, or test condition may have produced it.
What this tells the teacher:
This level shows whether students can move from “what happened” to “why it happened.” Strong evidence includes specific data, clear cause-and-effect reasoning, and accurate connections between design features and performance outcomes.
DOK 4 — Rungs 7–8

Format: Performance task, testing plan design, critique of an investigation, unfamiliar data scenario, engineering test report, or capstone testing protocol

Purpose: Check whether students can transfer ES3 skills by designing or critiquing testing systems and defending conclusions from evidence.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Design a Fair Testing Plan Can Be Assessed Before Design Your Own Challenge – Students design a testing procedure for an unfamiliar challenge, including variables, measurements, trials, and success criteria.
  2. Critique a Flawed Test Can Be Assessed After Multiple Major Challenges – Students identify problems in a testing setup and explain how the flaws could make the results unreliable.
  3. Engineering Test Report Mini-Task Can Be Assessed During Bridge, Glider, or Landing Rover – Students summarize procedure, data, analysis, conclusion, limitations, and next-step recommendations in a short technical format.
  4. Capstone Testing Protocol Can Be Assessed During Design Your Own Challenge – Students create the scoring system and testing protocol for their self-designed challenge and justify why the evidence will measure success fairly.
What this tells the teacher:
DOK 4 tasks show whether students can think beyond a teacher-provided test. Strong students can design fair tests, recognize limitations, defend evidence quality, and explain how data should drive engineering conclusions.

Summative Assessment Plan

Summative Evidence for ES3-II: Students demonstrate proficiency by producing an engineering artifact that shows how a prototype was built, how it was tested, what data was collected, how the results were analyzed, and what the evidence reveals about design effectiveness.

Recommended Artifact Options:

  • Engineering Test Report: A technical report documenting test procedure, data, observations, analysis, conclusions, and limitations.
  • Data Dashboard: A visual artifact using tables, graphs, trial summaries, and performance notes to communicate prototype results.
  • Testing Analysis Poster: A poster explaining the test setup, key results, design strengths, weaknesses, and evidence-based conclusions.
  • Demonstration Video: A recorded test demonstration with narration explaining how the prototype works, what was measured, and what the results show.
  • Engineering Journal Article: A written artifact that tells the story of the prototype test cycle using evidence, analysis, and technical explanation.
  • Prototype Performance Report: A concise artifact focused on performance metrics, reliability, failure points, and recommendations based on data.

Challenge Examples: Bridge Efficiency Test Report, Glider Flight Analysis Data Dashboard, Earthquake Tower Testing Analysis Poster, MagLev Momentum Performance Report, Landing Rover Drop Test Report, Water Rocket Distance Trial Data Report, or Design Your Own Challenge Testing Protocol.

Suggested Rubric Categories: prototype functionality, testing procedure, accuracy and completeness of data, use of multiple trials, organization of results, interpretation of evidence, analysis of strengths and weaknesses, explanation of performance, and clarity of technical communication.

Holistic Rubric

A — Highly Proficient

The student constructs a functional prototype, collects accurate and meaningful data, analyzes results with clear evidence, and explains performance using engineering reasoning and scientific principles. The artifact is organized, specific, and shows strong understanding of fair testing, reliability, limitations, and evidence-based conclusions.

B — Proficient

The student constructs a testable prototype, collects relevant data, organizes results, and explains how the evidence shows design effectiveness. The artifact is mostly complete and clear. Some analysis may lack depth, but conclusions are supported by data and connected to the challenge goals.

C — Developing Proficiency

The student shows partial understanding of building, testing, and analysis. The prototype may be testable and some data may be collected, but records may be incomplete, analysis may be basic, or conclusions may not fully connect to evidence. There is enough evidence that the student understands testing as part of the engineering process.

Not Yet — Insufficient Evidence

The student does not yet provide sufficient evidence of ES3-II proficiency. The artifact may lack a functional prototype, clear testing procedure, reliable data, meaningful analysis, or evidence-based conclusions. The student may need reteaching, guided testing support, data organization practice, or reassessment.
