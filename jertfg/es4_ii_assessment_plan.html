STEM II – ES4-II: Improve and Communicate Engineering Solutions

Essential Standard

Students will refine designs using evidence and communicate engineering decisions and outcomes in order to improve performance, explain the impact of design changes, and share engineering understanding with others.
Learning Target 1: Improving Engineering Designs

Learning Target: Students will use testing evidence, observations, feedback, and engineering reasoning to identify weaknesses and make meaningful design improvements.

Challenge Connections: Bridge, Glider, Earthquake Tower, MagLev Vehicle, Landing Rover, Water Rocket Distance

  • I can identify specific weaknesses, failure points, or limitations in a design.
  • I can use testing evidence and observations to decide what should be improved.
  • I can propose design changes that directly address performance problems.
  • I can revise a prototype while maintaining the challenge criteria and constraints.
  • I can explain why a design change should improve performance, reliability, safety, or efficiency.
Learning Target 2: Communicating Engineering Findings

Learning Target: Students will organize and communicate engineering decisions, evidence, results, and conclusions through clear artifacts designed for a specific audience.

Challenge Connections: Bridge, Glider, Earthquake Tower, MagLev Vehicle, Landing Rover, Water Rocket Distance, Design Your Own Challenge

  • I can organize engineering information so another person can understand the design process.
  • I can use visuals, data, sketches, photos, tables, or diagrams to support engineering explanations.
  • I can explain design decisions, trade-offs, test results, and improvements clearly.
  • I can choose an artifact format that fits the purpose of the engineering communication.
  • I can communicate conclusions using evidence instead of opinion or vague claims.
Learning Target 3: Reflecting on the Engineering Process

Learning Target: Students will reflect on successes, failures, teamwork, and decision-making in order to explain what was learned and how the process could improve in future challenges.

Challenge Connections: MagLev Vehicle, Landing Rover, Water Rocket Distance, Design Your Own Challenge

  • I can evaluate how well my team used the Engineering Design Process.
  • I can explain how failure, feedback, or unexpected results helped improve a design.
  • I can identify lessons learned about design, testing, teamwork, or communication.
  • I can describe how earlier decisions affected later prototype performance.
  • I can apply what I learned from one challenge to future engineering work.

Progress Tracking

Not Yet: I need support identifying useful improvements or explaining my engineering decisions.
Getting There: I can make improvements and communicate results, but I need more practice connecting changes to evidence.
Got It!: I can improve a design, explain why changes were made, and communicate engineering outcomes clearly using evidence.

Assessment Methods by DOK Level

DOK 1 — Rungs 1–2

Format: 3–5 minute warm-up, quick check, photo prompt, redesign prompt, reflection exit ticket, or artifact planning check

Purpose: Check whether students can recognize that designs improve through evidence, feedback, and iteration, and whether they can identify basic weaknesses or improvement opportunities.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Design Weakness Identification Can Be Assessed After Initial Testing – Students examine a prototype result or photo and identify one specific weakness that could be improved.
  2. Evidence or Opinion Sort Can Be Assessed During Any Major Challenge – Students sort statements into evidence-based conclusions and unsupported opinions about design performance.
  3. Improvement Match Can Be Assessed During Bridge, Glider, or Landing Rover – Students match common performance problems to reasonable improvement ideas.
  4. Communication Artifact Recognition Can Be Assessed Before Artifact Creation – Students identify which artifact type best fits a purpose, such as explaining failure, showing redesign, or presenting final results.
What this tells the teacher:
Students who struggle here may not yet understand iteration as a purposeful part of engineering. They may need support naming specific weaknesses, separating evidence from opinion, or understanding that communication artifacts should match the purpose and audience.
DOK 2 — Rungs 3–4

Format: Exit ticket, redesign note, artifact outline, before-and-after comparison, peer feedback response, or short written explanation

Purpose: Check whether students can make reasonable improvements, organize findings, and explain basic design changes using evidence from testing.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Before-and-After Redesign Note Can Be Assessed During Any Redesign Day – Students describe the original issue, the change made, and the evidence that motivated the change.
  2. MagLev Improvement Plan Can Be Assessed During MagLev Vehicle – Students use velocity, mass, cargo, stability, or consistency evidence to propose one targeted design modification.
  3. Landing Rover Communication Outline Can Be Assessed Before Landing Rover Artifact – Students outline a report, video, or poster showing how their rover protected the egg and what the results show.
  4. Artifact Evidence Check Can Be Assessed Before Final Submission – Students identify which visuals, data, and observations should be included to support their conclusions.
What this tells the teacher:
Students at this level can usually describe a change, but the assessment reveals whether the change is connected to evidence. Weak responses often list random modifications, omit test results, or communicate what they built without explaining why decisions were made.
DOK 3 — Rungs 5–6

Format: Constructed response, failure analysis, redesign justification, peer review response, artifact critique, CER-style explanation, or group presentation defense

Purpose: Check whether students can justify improvements and communication choices using data, feedback, constraints, and engineering reasoning.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Failure Analysis Response Can Be Assessed During Bridge, Earthquake Tower, or Landing Rover – Students explain what failed, why it likely failed, and what design change would directly address the cause.
  2. Redesign Justification CER Can Be Assessed During Any Major Challenge – Students make a claim about the best improvement, support it with evidence, and explain the engineering reasoning.
  3. Artifact Critique and Revision Can Be Assessed During Artifact Work Time – Students critique a draft artifact for clarity, evidence, organization, and audience fit, then revise it.
  4. Process Reflection Analysis Can Be Assessed After MagLev, Landing Rover, or Water Rocket Distance – Students analyze how one team decision helped or hurt the final outcome and explain what they would do differently next time.
What this tells the teacher:
This level shows whether students can move from “we changed it” to “we changed it because the evidence showed…” Strong responses connect specific results to design choices, acknowledge trade-offs, and explain how communication choices make the engineering story clearer.
DOK 4 — Rungs 7–8

Format: Performance task, redesign portfolio, critique task, public artifact, engineering showcase, capstone reflection, or design-transfer prompt

Purpose: Check whether students can transfer ES4 skills by independently evaluating, improving, communicating, and reflecting on engineering work in complex or unfamiliar contexts.

Assessment Ideas

  1. Redesign Portfolio Mini-Task Can Be Assessed After Multiple Rounds of Testing – Students document version 1, evidence gathered, version 2 changes, retest results, and conclusions about improvement.
  2. Critique a Redesign Proposal Can Be Assessed After Any Major Challenge – Students evaluate a proposed improvement and determine whether the evidence actually supports the redesign.
  3. Engineering Communication Transfer Task Can Be Assessed Before Design Your Own Challenge – Students choose the best artifact format for a new engineering situation and defend how it will communicate the process and results.
  4. Capstone Engineering Showcase Plan Can Be Assessed During Design Your Own Challenge – Students plan how they will communicate the problem, design decisions, testing evidence, redesigns, and final conclusions for their self-designed challenge.
What this tells the teacher:
DOK 4 tasks show whether students can independently use evidence to improve and communicate engineering work. Strong students can critique redesign logic, select communication strategies intentionally, and explain how lessons from one challenge transfer to future engineering problems.

Summative Assessment Plan

Summative Evidence for ES4-II: Students demonstrate proficiency by producing an engineering artifact that shows how evidence, feedback, and reflection were used to improve a design and communicate engineering decisions clearly.

Recommended Artifact Options:

  • Redesign Portfolio: A before-and-after artifact showing prototype versions, testing evidence, design changes, retesting results, and conclusions.
  • Failure Analysis Report: A technical explanation of what failed, why it failed, what was changed, and whether the redesign improved performance.
  • Design Evolution Poster: A visual artifact showing how the solution changed over time and how evidence guided each major decision.
  • Reflection Video: A recorded reflection explaining design choices, teamwork, failures, improvements, and lessons learned from the challenge.
  • Technical Article: A written artifact that communicates the engineering story, evidence, redesign decisions, and final conclusions for a broader audience.
  • Engineering Showcase Presentation: A polished communication artifact that explains the full design journey, including problem, solution, testing, redesign, and final results.

Challenge Examples: Bridge Redesign Portfolio, Glider Design Evolution Poster, Earthquake Tower Failure Analysis Report, MagLev Reflection Video, Landing Rover Technical Article, Water Rocket Distance Engineering Showcase, or Design Your Own Challenge Capstone Presentation.

Suggested Rubric Categories: identification of design weaknesses, use of evidence to guide improvements, quality of redesign reasoning, connection between changes and performance, clarity of communication, effective use of visuals and data, reflection on process, and transfer of learning to future challenges.

Holistic Rubric

A — Highly Proficient

The student clearly identifies design weaknesses, uses strong evidence to guide meaningful improvements, explains how redesign decisions affected performance, and communicates engineering findings with clarity and purpose. The artifact is well-organized, evidence-rich, reflective, and demonstrates strong understanding of iteration, communication, and engineering decision-making.

B — Proficient

The student identifies reasonable design improvements, supports decisions with evidence, and communicates results clearly. The artifact explains the redesign process and includes relevant data, visuals, or observations. Some reasoning may lack depth, but the main conclusions are connected to the challenge goals and evidence.

C — Developing Proficiency

The student shows partial understanding of improvement and communication. The artifact may describe changes or results, but the connection to evidence may be limited, unclear, or incomplete. Communication may focus more on what was built than why decisions were made, but there is enough evidence that the student understands iteration as part of engineering.

Not Yet — Insufficient Evidence

The student does not yet provide sufficient evidence of ES4-II proficiency. The artifact may lack a clear improvement process, evidence-based reasoning, meaningful reflection, or organized communication. The student may need reteaching, guided revision, peer feedback support, or reassessment.
